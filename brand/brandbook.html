02 — The problem

The old site was not badly designed.
It was unfinished, in public.

Nothing below is a matter of taste. Every item is a factual defect that a visitor could see, a search engine could index, or a translator could not touch. Listed plainly, they explain why a rebrand is the cheap option.

Defect 01 · Identity

A 184×55 pixel palm tree

The logo shipped as a single raster PNG at 184×55 px. No vector original, no monochrome variant, no clear-space rule, no minimum size. Its palm fronds were hairlines — at favicon size they dissolved into grey mush, and on any dark surface they disappeared entirely. A logo that cannot be scaled cannot be printed, embroidered, etched into a shopfront, or rendered at 16 px.

Old · hairline raster

New · solid geometry

Defect 02 · Structure

The headline was a JPEG

The homepage hero baked its headline text into a photograph. That single decision costs four things at once: the sentence cannot be translated into ka / en / ru, it is invisible to search engines, it cannot reflow on a phone, and a screen reader announces nothing. A shop whose main promise exists only as pixels is a shop with no main promise.

Defect 03 · Content

“dfdfdf” went live

The About page and the Service page both shipped with keyboard-mash placeholder text still in the body. The Service page is where a customer decides whether to trust you with a 3,199₾ phone. It said dfdfdf.

Doubled protocols in image URLs

Product and theme images were emitted with the protocol written twice and the path doubled — a template variable concatenated onto an already-absolute base. Every one of those URLs is a broken image, a 404 in the server log, and a slower page.

Defect 05 · Trust

Social links pointed at another brand

The footer's social icons resolved to chvenebi.ge — an unrelated business. A customer who clicks “Facebook” on an Apple reseller and lands somewhere else does not conclude “broken link”. They conclude “not a real shop”.

Defect 06 · Pricing

A struck-through 0₾

Accessory cards rendered an “old price” of 0₾ with a line through it, because the field was empty and the template printed it anyway. It reads as either a bug or a lie about a discount. Both are expensive on a page whose only job is to be believed about money.

Defect 07 · Confidence

“The site is running in test mode”

A sitewide banner told every visitor the shop was not really open. It is the most honest thing on the old site and the most damaging. A customer comparing four Apple resellers does not need a reason to leave — they need one to stay.

Defect 08 · Typography

Three unrelated typefaces

FiraGO for Georgian, SF Pro Light for Latin, Poppins for headings. Three skeletons, three x-heights, three sets of digits. Georgian and Latin never aligned on the same baseline, and a price could change face mid-string when the lari sign fell out of the subset.

FiraGO SF Pro Light Poppins

What the list adds up to

Individually these are bugs. Together they are a position: this shop is provisional. Every defect above transmits the same message — that the numbers might not be right, that the pages are not finished, that somebody else's brand is in the footer. iLand sells devices that cost between two and five thousand lari, on instalment plans that run for two years. The entire purchase is an act of trust extended before delivery.

So the brand's job is not to look better. It is to look finished, and be checkable. That is the whole strategy in section 03.

03 — Positioning

An island is not a metaphor.
It is an editing decision.

iLand's name already contains its strategy. An island is a bounded place — small, chosen, finite, and calm because of it. In a market split between greyscale Apple imitations and shouting marketplaces, the open position is the quiet one.

The competitive map

Georgia's Apple retail market sorts into two camps, and neither of them occupies the middle. On one side, the specialists — iSpace, iSTYLE, iStore — render themselves as greyscale apple.com clones: white field, thin grey type, borrowed product photography, no colour of their own. They look credible and interchangeable. You cannot tell them apart in a screenshot with the logo cropped out.

On the other side, the generalists — Zoommer, Alta, Extra — are electronics marketplaces where Apple is one aisle among refrigerators. Zoommer owns a cheap violet; Alta a big-box red grid. They are loud, dense, discount-led, and they win on price alone.

The gap between them is a curated specialist with a colour and a conscience about numbers. That is the whole opening, and it is unoccupied.

The proof mechanism: publish the numbers

Positioning without proof is a slogan. iLand's proof is a policy: every number that a competitor hides, iLand prints. Prices with the monthly განვადება figure beside them. Repair costs. Turnaround hours. Warranty months. Stock counts. Delivery windows.

This is not a general-purpose virtue — it is aimed at one specific, verifiable gap. No Apple retailer in Georgia publishes repair prices. iSpace's service page is a bare navigation hub: categories, no figures, no turnaround, no warranty terms. The customer's only route to a number is to physically carry a broken phone to Saburtalo and ask. iLand answers the question on the page: ეკრანის შეცვლა — 189₾-დან, 1 დღე, 6 თვე გარანტია.

Publishing is also a constraint on the business, which is the point. You cannot print a turnaround you do not hit twice.

Positioning statement

Georgia's Apple island — one curated shelf, one honest price with განვადება shown up front, and the same people who sell it are the people who repair it.

Audience

The considered upgrader

28–45, Tbilisi, replacing a three-year-old iPhone or Mac. Knows exactly which model they want and is shopping for terms, not for advice. Decides on the monthly figure, the delivery window, and whether the shop looks like it will still exist for the warranty.

The broken-screen arrival

Any age. Arrives from a search for a repair price at the worst moment of their week. Wants one number and one date. Converts to a buyer later, but only if the repair was quoted honestly and hit the printed turnaround.

The gift buyer

Buying for someone else, often not an Apple user themselves. Needs the shelf to be small enough to choose from, the price to include everything, and the box to look like it cost what it cost.

What the island commits us to

One shelf

Eight categories, edited. If a product needs a paragraph of justification, it does not go on the shelf.

One price

The number on the card is the number at checkout, with the monthly figure beside it.

One roof

The workshop is behind the same door as the till. Sold here, fixed here.

One voice

Georgian first, written natively. English and Russian are equals, never afterthoughts.

04 — Voice and tone

Say the number instead of the adjective.

iLand's voice is plain, specific, numerate and unhurried. It never shouts, never exclaims, and never uses an intensifier where a figure would do. If a sentence cannot be checked, it probably should not be written.

Principle 01

Numerate before persuasive

“Great value” is an opinion the reader has to accept. “2,252₾, or 188₾ a month for 12 months, TBC 0%” is a fact they can act on. Every claim should carry its own evidence in the same breath.

Principle 02

Unhurried

No countdown urgency, no “hurry”, no manufactured scarcity. Real scarcity is allowed because it is true: ბოლო ცალია when stock is genuinely one. The urgency belongs to the fact, not to the typography.

Principle 03

Georgian first, written natively

Georgian is the primary language, not a translation layer. Copy is written in Georgian and then written again in English and Russian — never run through a translator. A native reader detects a translated sentence instantly, and a translated sentence undoes every honesty claim the brand makes.

Principle 04

No exclamation marks

Not one, anywhere, in any language. This is a hard rule rather than a preference, because it is the single fastest way to sound like the marketplace competitors. If a sentence needs an exclamation mark to land, the sentence is wrong.

Three mechanical rules for writers

1. Currency is a suffix, always. 2,252₾ — never ₾2,252. Thousands take a comma. The lari sign never takes a space before it.
2. Sentence case everywhere. Buttons, badges and eyebrows are written in sentence case so that Georgian, English and Russian look identical in the same component. Georgian has no uppercase to fall back on — see section 07.
3. Emphasis is weight, not slant. Bold or semibold for emphasis in all three languages. Italic is never used in Georgian, and mixed italic/roman across languages breaks the visual system.

05 — The mark

A disc above a horizon, with its own reflection.

The mark reads two ways at once, and both readings are the brand. A sun setting into the sea — an island seen from the water. And the tittle and stem of a lowercase i. It is built from one circle and two rounded bars, and nothing else.

Why solid geometry

The old logo failed at exactly one thing, and it failed completely: stroke weight. A raster palm tree drawn in hairlines has no small size. This mark is drawn only in bars of 2.6 and 2.8 units against a 40 unit box — a stroke-to-box ratio of roughly 1:15. It survives a 16 px favicon, an embroidered polo, and an etched brass plate, because there is nothing thin in it to lose.

The two readings

Island: the disc is a low sun, the long bar is the waterline, the short bar is the reflection on the water. Letterform: the disc is the tittle of an i, the bars are its stem laid flat. Neither reading is decoration — the reflection is what makes the horizon a sea horizon rather than a generic underline, and it is the first thing people try to delete. Do not delete it above 32 px.

The disc draws itself, once

Variants

Six sanctioned versions. Full colour is the default and should be used wherever the substrate allows it. Monochrome exists for single-ink print, embroidery, etching and fax-grade reproduction — not as a style choice.

Misuse

Each tile below is a real rendering of the wrong thing, not a description of it. None of these is hypothetical — every one is a mistake that a template, a slide deck or a sign-maker will make unless it is named here.

StretchedThe disc must stay a circle. Non-uniform scaling turns the sun into an eye and thins the horizontal strokes against the vertical ones.

RotatedA horizon is horizontal by definition. Tilt it and the mark stops being a seascape and starts being a logo doing a trick.

RecolouredThe disc is --lume and nothing else. Borrowing a competitor's violet is the single fastest way to look like the marketplace we are not.

Drop-shadowed or glowingThe mark is flat. Bevels, glows, outer shadows and “neon” effects all read as 2011, and none of them survive one-ink printing.

On a busy backgroundThe mark needs a plain field inside its clear space. Over photography or pattern, place it on a solid --abyss plate first.

Hairline-isedThinning the strokes “for elegance” recreates the exact failure of the old palm-tree logo. Weights are 2.6 and 2.8 at every size.

Gradient-filledThe disc is an outline, not a blob, and it takes flat colour only. A filled disc also destroys the lowercase-i reading.

Reflection deletedWithout the short bar there is no water, and the mark collapses into a generic “circle over a line”. Only the 32 px favicon may drop it.

06 — Colour

A night sea, lit at the waterline.

Nobody in the Georgian Apple market owns a colour except Zoommer, and that one reads cheap. The specialists are all greyscale. So iLand takes the position none of them will: a deep, near-black ground with a single bioluminescent accent, plus a warm horizon orange reserved strictly for offers.

Audit — resolved

Five corrections, applied in tokens.css

Every pair in the table above now clears 4.5:1. It did not when this section was first measured — the audit found five failures, all of them in light mode or in tertiary text, and all five were fixed in the tokens rather than waived. The ratios below are the real computed values, before and after.

#What failedWasFix appliedNow
1 Light-mode accent, as text on sand. The token comment claimed 4.6:1. It measured 3.58:1. 3.58:1 --lume-700 darkened from #14957C to #12806A. 4.66:1
2 White on the light-mode accent button. The primary CTA label, at normal size. 3.74:1 Same token change carries the button — no separate value needed. 4.86:1
3 White on the light-mode promo. Sale badges are small text, so the large-text relief does not apply. 3.87:1 --sun-700 darkened from #C46A18 to #A85610. 5.25:1
4 Dark-mode tertiary text on a card. Used by product taglines, category counts and footer links — body text, not decoration. 4.27:1 --text-3 lightened from #6B7B8F to #768396. 4.80:1
5 Light-mode tertiary text on sand. The same role, the other theme. 4.40:1 --ink-3 darkened from #6B7688 to #66707F. 4.80:1

The rule this leaves behind: a colour is not approved because it looks right on the swatch sheet. It is approved because the pair it will actually be used in measures 4.5:1 or better. Three of these five failures were within 0.3 of passing, which is exactly the range the eye cannot judge.

Rules that follow from the audit

Never put --lume #2FD6B4 on a light ground. It measures 1.77:1 on sand — invisible. Light mode must swap to the darker step; that swap is what :root[data-theme='light'] exists for.
Never put --sun #FF9F45 on a light ground as text. 1.95:1. Orange on white is a fill colour with dark text on it, never a text colour.
Hairlines are not text. --line at 1.36:1 on the page base is intentional — it is a boundary, not information. Anything a user must read or operate needs 4.5:1 or 3:1 respectively. The focus ring uses --accent at 10.95:1 precisely because it is operable.
Colour never carries meaning alone. Stock states pair colour with a word (მარაგშია / მცირე მარაგი / ბოლო ცალია), and sale prices pair orange with a struck-through original and a percentage.

Setting Mkhedruli: eight rules

These are the rules that separate a Georgian-native page from a translated one. A native reader spots a violation in under a second — usually without being able to say why, which is worse, because the feeling attaches to the brand rather than to the typography. Every WRONG panel below is really rendering the wrong way, in your browser, right now.

01  Mkhedruli has no uppercase. Never transform case.

Georgian is unicameral in everyday use. Unicode 11 did add a case mapping from Mkhedruli to Mtavruli (U+1C90–U+1CBA), so text-transform: uppercase is not a no-op — it swaps in a completely different set of letterforms. Mtavruli is a real style used for signage and titling, but it is not a case pair: Georgians do not write mixed Mtavruli/Mkhedruli, and no Georgian interface labels its buttons in it. Worse, if your webfont subset omits the Mtavruli block — which most subsetters do by default — every transformed label renders as empty boxes. And text-transform: capitalize is pure nonsense: it converts only the first letter, producing a word no one has ever written.

Wrong

კალათაში დამატება

text-transform: uppercase — this is Mtavruli, not emphasis.

კალათაში დამატება

text-transform: capitalize — one Mtavruli letter glued to a Mkhedruli word.

Right

კალათაში დამატება

Sentence case, untransformed. Scope every caps utility to :lang(en) and :lang(ru).

:lang(en) .eyebrow,
:lang(ru) .eyebrow { text-transform: uppercase;
                    letter-spacing: .10em }
:lang(ka) .eyebrow { text-transform: none;
                    letter-spacing: 0 }

Design consequence: every button, badge and eyebrow is designed in sentence case, so all three languages look identical in the same component. There is no “caps version” of the UI to fall back on.

02  Never italic. Emphasise with weight.

Georgian has no italic tradition and Noto Sans Georgian ships no Georgian italic. Ask for one and the browser synthesises it by shearing the upright — which natives read as a rendering fault, not as emphasis. The round forms ო, ფ, ღ, ძ distort worst.

Wrong

ფასი, რომელიც არ იმალება — ღონისძიება ძველ ოფისში.

font-style: italic — synthesised oblique. Notice ღ, ო and ძ.

Right

ფასი, რომელიც არ იმალება — ღონისძიება ძველ ოფისში.

font-weight: 600. Already enforced: :lang(ka) em, :lang(ka) i { font-style: normal; font-weight: 600 }.

03  Never negative letter-spacing.

Tight tracking is a Latin display trick — it works because Latin capitals are mostly straight-sided. Mkhedruli is built from round counters, and negative tracking closes them: ო, ფ, ღ, ძ and ბ collapse into blots and words turn to mush. Latin display can take −0.02 to −0.04em. Georgian display must be 0.

Wrong

ფოტოგრაფია ღონისძიება

letter-spacing: -0.05em — the counters of ფ, ო, ღ and ძ are closing.

Right

ფოტოგრაფია ღონისძიება

letter-spacing: 0. The token system already does this in :lang(ka) .display.

Latin display — tight is correct

Everything Apple

Georgian display — same step, zero tracking, weight 700

04  More size, more leading.

Mkhedruli's body height sits between Latin x-height and cap-height, so at an identical font-size it reads smaller and lighter — it needs about +5%. And its tall ascenders (ბ ზ ფ ჰ ლ) and deep descenders (გ ყ ც ძ ჭ ჯ ღ) collide at Latin leading. Body copy goes to 1.65 against Latin's 1.5; display to 1.15–1.25 against Latin's 1.02–1.1.

Wrong

ბზფჰლ — გყცძჭჯღ. ჩვენი სერვისი იმავე კართან არის, სადაც იყიდე. ეკრანი ერთ დღეში, ორიგინალი ნაწილით, წერილობითი დასკვნით.

font-size: 1em; line-height: 1.32 — Latin settings. Ascenders in one line touch descenders in the line above.

Right

ბზფჰლ — გყცძჭჯღ. ჩვენი სერვისი იმავე კართან არის, სადაც იყიდე. ეკრანი ერთ დღეში, ორიგინალი ნაწილით, წერილობითი დასკვნით.

font-size: 1.05em; line-height: 1.65 — the :lang(ka) defaults.

05  Small caps are meaningless.

font-variant-caps: small-caps and its relatives have nothing to operate on in a unicameral script. The browser fakes them by scaling Mtavruli forms or by shrinking the originals, and the result looks like a rendering bug. The token file neutralises this with font-variant-caps: normal on :lang(ka).

Wrong

გამოქვეყნებული ფასები

Either nothing happens — in which case the declaration is a lie in the stylesheet — or the browser fakes it.

Right

გამოქვეყნებული ფასები

Use weight and colour for hierarchy. Reserve small caps for :lang(en) if you use them at all.

06  The lari sign lives in latin-ext. Never subset it away.

₾ is U+20BE, and it is in the latin-ext subset — not the georgian subset, where an optimiser looking at a Georgian site would reasonably expect to find it. Load only georgian and every price on the site silently falls back to a system font mid-string: the digits are Noto and the currency sign is whatever the OS has. It is the most common and least noticed typographic bug on Georgian e-commerce.

Wrong — latin-ext dropped

Right — latin-ext included

3,199₾

One face, one weight, one baseline. Verify after every font-loading change by rendering a price.

Currency is always a suffix with no space: 3,199₾. Thousands take a comma.

07  Prices and spec tables use tabular lining figures.

Georgian has no digits of its own and uses Western Arabic numerals, so the price and its Georgian label are set by the same face — there is no mid-string face jump to worry about, but there is a width problem. With proportional figures, 1 is narrower than 8 and a column of prices will not align, and a live-updating instalment figure will jitter as the digits change.

09 — Motion

Six curves, and every one of them has a job.

Motion is a state change made legible, not decoration. Six named curves and four durations cover the entire product. A seventh curve is not a taste problem, it is a sign that a transition has not been decided yet. Reduced motion is written into the components, never bolted on with a global reset.

The six curves

Each plot below is the real cubic-bezier, drawn from 0 to 1 with its two control handles. Each track underneath actually runs on that curve. All six loops travel for the same 990 ms — four to seven times slower than the values they ship at — so the only thing that differs between them is the shape. Ship the numbers in the band, not the demo speed.

--e-quiet

cubic-bezier(.40, 0, .20, 1)

Band 140 – 220 ms

The default. Every state change that has no reason to be anything else: hover colour, border, focus ring, a chip toggling. Symmetrical, so nothing draws attention to itself.

--e-reveal

cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .30, 1)

Band 480 – 760 ms

Entrances only. It covers 90% of its distance in the first third of the time, so an element arrives fast and settles slowly — the thing that makes an entrance read as arriving rather than sliding.

--e-swift

cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)

Band 180 – 260 ms

Direct response to a pointer: the card lift, the button translate, a dropdown opening under the cursor. A shorter, blunter --e-reveal. Anything the user caused should use this one.

--e-exit

cubic-bezier(.55, 0, 1, .45)

Band 110 – 460 ms

The only accelerating curve in the set, and the only one allowed on a departure. It is still moving at its fastest when it leaves the frame, which is what makes a dismissal feel decided.

--e-pop

cubic-bezier(.34, 1.56, .64, 1)

Band 220 – 320 ms

Peaks at 1.098 of the target and comes back. Confirmations only — the tick after a cart add, the saved state on a wishlist heart. Never on a figure: see the money rule below.

--e-cinematic

cubic-bezier(.83, 0, .17, 1)

Band 480 – 760 ms

Steep in the middle, near-still at both ends. Reserved for moves the size of the viewport: a full-bleed gallery step, the view transition between shelf and product. Two per session, not two per screen.

Duration is a function of distance

Four durations, and one rule for choosing between them: how far the thing moves. A 4 px lift over 480 ms looks broken. A 480 px drawer over 140 ms looks like a rendering glitch. Under 40 px take --t-fast; 40 to 200 px take --t-base; over 200 px take --t-slow; and only a page-scale move earns --t-reveal, once per page load.

An ease-out exit decelerates into nothing, so the element loiters at the point where it should already be gone — the user reads it as hesitation, or as the site not having registered the click. Reduced motion: the reset that looks responsible and is a bug

This is the snippet copied into most sites, and it is the reason some people who turn the accessibility setting on get a worse page than everyone else. The policy here is the opposite shape: resting states are authored visible, and motion is removed component by component. If an element is only visible because an animation ran, the element is authored wrong, and no media query can fix that.

Never

/* the blanket reset */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *, *::before, *::after {
    animation: none !important;
    transition: none !important;
  }
}

1. It permanently hides content. Any reveal whose resting state is opacity: 0 — every scroll-in block, every staggered grid — is animated to opacity: 1. Kill the animation and the element stays at zero forever. The page is not merely stiller, it is missing, and it is missing only for the users who asked for less movement.

2. It breaks discrete transitions. A dialog or popover closing with transition-behavior: allow-discrete relies on the transition running so that display: none and the overlay property are applied at the end of it. With transition: none the element never un-renders: it stays in the top layer, invisible, swallowing every click behind it.

3. It kills non-motion animation too. animation: none takes out the indeterminate progress indicator that was carrying state, and any transitionend or .finished promise a component was waiting on never resolves.

Ship this

/* tokens.css — durations collapse, states do not */
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  :root {
    --t-fast: 1ms; --t-base: 1ms;
    --t-slow: 1ms; --t-reveal: 1ms;
  }

  /* only the ambient loops actually stop */
  .ambient, .marquee__track, .aurora {
    animation: none !important;
  }

  /* reveals are forced to their finished state */
  [data-reveal] {
    opacity: 1 !important;
    transform: none !important;
    clip-path: none !important;
  }
}

1 ms is not 0. A 1 ms transition is imperceptible and still fires: transitionend arrives, allow-discrete completes its display: none, and any JS awaiting animation.finished continues. Setting the duration to zero would reintroduce the same class of bug from the other direction.

The list is short on purpose. Three ambient selectors and one reveal attribute cover the whole site, because ambient motion is the only motion authored to run without a user asking for it. Everything else is a state change the user triggered, and a state change still has to happen.

The six loops in this section are switched off by exactly this rule. The plots stay, because the shape of the curve is the information and the movement was only ever an illustration of it.

Nine things this brand does not do

Each of these is common enough on Georgian commerce sites to be a default. Each one costs either frames, trust, or a customer who cannot complete a purchase.

transition: all plus blanket will-change

all animates properties nobody intended — including layout ones like height, which run on the main thread — and a permanent will-change promotes every node to its own compositor layer, so the optimisation meant to save frames is what spends the memory that drops them.

Fade-up on scroll, applied to everything

When every block enters identically the animation stops meaning “this is new” and starts meaning “this page is slow”; the AOS carpet-bomb also delays the first readable paint of content that was already downloaded. One reveal per screen, on the element that earns it.

Scroll hijacking

It breaks the scrollbar's contract with the page and takes PageDown, Home, End, find-in-page and every assistive technology that scrolls by a fixed amount with it — the visitor loses the one control they had, on a site whose whole claim is that it is straightforward.

Any response to a tap slower than 250 ms

Past roughly 100 ms the tap stops feeling connected to the result, and past 250 ms the user taps a second time and the shop books two of something; press feedback belongs at --t-fast and must be independent of whatever network call the tap started.

Springy overshoot on money, stock or legal copy

--e-pop crosses its target by 9.8% of the distance travelled and returns, so a price counting from 0 to 2,899₾ passes through 3,182₾ on the way — a figure that bounces is a figure the reader stops trusting, and this brand's entire position is published numbers.

Full-screen preloaders on a storefront

A shop that hides its shelf behind a spinning logo has added its own animation to the load time it was trying to disguise; render the grid, let the images arrive into reserved boxes, and never block the catalogue on a decoration.

Parallax on the hero product

The product has to be the one object on the page that is exactly where it appears to be, because a customer is deciding whether to spend 2,899₾ on it; move the background plate behind it if the depth is worth having, never the device.

Auto-advancing carousels with no pause control

WCAG 2.2.2 requires a real mechanism to pause anything that moves, blinks or scrolls for more than 5 seconds, and pause-on-hover is not a mechanism on a phone, where hover does not exist — which is where most of this traffic is.

A site-wide custom cursor

It removes the I-beam that tells a person text can be selected and the pointer that tells them a thing can be clicked, then adds a frame of latency to the single element on screen that must never lag behind the hand moving it.

One more, from tokens.css and adjacent to all nine: scroll-behavior: smooth is never set on html. It desynchronises the scrollbar from the document, breaks keyboard paging, and corrupts scroll-timeline measurements. Opt in per anchor instead.

Chips

Two states, one attribute

The selected state is styled from [aria-pressed="true"], not from a class. One source of truth: it is impossible to render a state that the screen reader is not announcing, which is the usual way a filter ends up visually on and silently off. Transition is --t-base --e-quiet on colour and border only — chips never move.

The price block

This is the component the whole brand rests on. One rule governs it: the struck price renders only when there is a genuinely higher price to strike — a real number, strictly greater than the current price, that was actually charged. The percentage is derived from those two numbers and is never entered by a human.

12 — Do and don't

Twelve ways to keep it, twelve ways to lose it.

Most of these are not matters of taste. They are the specific failures that made the previous site look unfinished, plus the specific failures that make a Georgian site look like it was translated rather than written.

Set Georgian in sentence case, always

Mkhedruli is unicameral — there is no uppercase. Scope text-transform to :lang(en) and :lang(ru), and design every button, badge and eyebrow as sentence case so all three languages look identical.

Don't fake capitals on ქართული

A global text-transform:uppercase is the single most common way a Georgian site looks broken. It is either a silent no-op or it produces Mtavruli in fonts that map it — neither is what the designer intended.

Emphasise Georgian with weight 600

Noto Sans Georgian ships no Georgian italic, so weight is the only honest emphasis available.

Don't italicise Mkhedruli

font-style:italic makes the browser synthesise a mechanically sheared Mkhedruli. Native readers see a rendering bug, not emphasis.

Track Latin tight, Georgian at zero

Latin display takes -0.038em. Georgian display takes 0, and Georgian body can take a hair positive.

Don't inherit negative tracking into ქართული

Tight tracking closes the round counters of ო, ფ, ღ and ძ, and the word turns to mush at exactly the sizes where the headline matters most.

Ship latin + latin-ext in every subset

The lari sign ₾ is U+20BE and lives in latin-ext. Add 1 369 ₾ to the font smoke test, in every weight and every fallback.

Don't subset the webfont to “georgian” only

Do that and every price on the site silently falls back to a system font — the one string on the page that must never look accidental.

Spend Lume on the one action

One primary accent per screen: the thing you want tapped. Roughly 5% of the pixels, and it keeps meaning “act here”.

Don't decorate with the accent

Tint every border, icon and heading with #2FD6B4 and the CTA stops reading as a CTA. An accent used everywhere is a background colour.

Let prices arrive instantly

Price, stock state and the add-to-cart button are rendered at their final value the moment the pixel lands. The instalment figure may count up; it must never overshoot.

Don't animate money or warnings

A bouncing 1,369₾ or a wobbling ბოლო ცალია reads as a toy. Overshoot belongs to confirmations — the cart badge, the wishlist heart.

Strike a price only when one exists

Render the was-price when, and only when, there is a genuinely higher figure to strike. Otherwise print the price alone.

Don't print a struck-through 0₾

The old site did this on every accessory. One detail like it costs more trust than any amount of visual polish can win back.

Set hero copy in the DOM

Live text is translatable into ka/en/ru, readable by search engines and screen readers, and responsive by default.

Don't bake headlines into a JPEG

The previous hero was a flat image with English, Georgian and a script “Champions Sale!” burned into the pixels. It could not be translated, indexed, resized or read aloud.

Treat placeholders as build blockers

No page ships while it still contains filler. Grep for it before deploy the same way you would grep for a leftover console.log.

Don't let “dfdfdf” reach production

That exact string was live on the About and Service pages. A visitor who sees it concludes, correctly, that nobody has looked at the page.

Build the mark from solid geometry

A circle and two rounded-cap bars, minimum 2.6 units of stroke. It survives 16px, one-colour printing, embroidery and a laser-etched sign.

Don't reintroduce hairlines

The old palm-tree mark failed precisely here: fine strokes in a 184×55 raster that vanished at favicon size and clogged at sign size.

Pair every colour with a word

Stock state is a coloured dot and a label — მარაგშია, მცირე მარაგი, ბოლო ცალია. The label is what actually carries the meaning.

Don't let colour be the only signal

Roughly one in twelve men has a colour vision deficiency. A red dot alone does not tell them the last unit is going.

Suppress instalments below 100₾

Grey the module and say so: ონლაინ განვადება მოქმედებს 100₾-დან. A third of the catalogue sits under that floor.

Don't quote a monthly figure you cannot honour

Offering “2₾ / თვეში” on a 19₾ screen protector is a promise the bank will refuse — and the customer finds out at the checkout, not on the card.
